  - q: Should I replace fascias and soffits at the same time as roof work?

## Where roofs actually leak

Most roof leaks do not come through the tiles. They come through the lead: a
flashing that has split or pulled away, a soaker that was never fitted, a valley worn
thin, a chimney apron dressed too short. Lead detailing is skilled work, and it is where
cheap roofing fails first. Sealant and stick-on flashing tape are sold as fixes; neither
lasts, and both make the proper repair harder when it finally comes.

The other weak point is the roofline. Timber fascias rot from the top edge, often where
the felt behind the gutter has perished and drips onto the board. Once the timber
softens, gutter brackets lose their fixing, joints open up and water runs down the wall.

## Our approach

Lead moves with temperature, so it has to be fixed in a way that lets it. We use the
correct code of lead for each detail, laid in short lengths with proper laps, dressed
into the masonry joints, then wedged and pointed.
Chimneys get an apron, stepped flashings and a back gutter formed properly. Abutments on
slate and plain tile get individual soakers under a cover flashing. Valleys are
stripped, the boards checked, and the new lead laid to correct laps and lengths.

On the roofline, we strip back to sound timber, replace anything rotten, and fit uPVC
fascias and soffits with roof space ventilation maintained. New guttering goes on at the
right fall, with outlets where the water actually needs to go.

## Worth doing while the scaffold is up

Older houses across Leeds often still carry their original timber roofline, and
boards of that age are usually paint over soft wood. If we are already scaffolded for a
re-roof or a repair, renewing the fascias, soffits and gutters at the same time is the
cheapest it will ever be. We price it as a separate item so you can take it or leave it.
